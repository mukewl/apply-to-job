# Outreach rules

Two things per person: a connection note sent with the request, and a message sent once
they accept. Both are drafted for the applicant to send **by hand**. Nothing is ever
sent automatically and no account is ever logged into.

---

## The connection note — under 300 characters

One line on what they applied for, one line on who they are. No metrics. No pitch.

> Hi Patrick - I've applied for the Activation Performance & Tracking Manager role at
> GROHE. I come from paid media and ended up owning the measurement side of it. Would
> like to connect.

## The message — under 140 words

1. `Hi <Name>, thanks for connecting.`
2. The honest reason for writing: they've applied, and would **rather ask someone
   inside than work it out from the job ad**.
3. Two or three sentences on who they are, tied to something specific about **that
   person's** background. No numbers.
4. One or two real questions. What the team is actually like day to day, plus something
   only that person could answer about their own path or the work.
5. `Would you have 15 minutes for a call in the next week or two? Happy to fit around
   whenever suits you.`
6. `Thanks,` / `<Name>`

Budget note: the greeting, the "rather ask someone inside" line, the closing ask and the
sign-off cost about 58 words between them. That leaves roughly 82 for the middle.

---

## Never ask for a referral

Not "if you could flag my name", not "a word to whoever is hiring would help", not
"happy to be introduced". **The ask is always 15 minutes for a conversation.**

It puts a stranger on the spot. Vouching for someone they have never spoken to carries
reputational risk, so the honest answer is no and the easy answer is silence. It also
converts what could have been a conversation into a favour they must decline.

This holds when the contact is already a connection, when they are senior enough to make
it happen with one message, and when the applicant is clearly qualified. The only thing
that changes with seniority is the **question** they bring — never the ask.

Asking someone to **point you to who owns an area** is different from asking them to
introduce or recommend you, and is acceptable when a contact has said they cannot help
directly. Judge it by whether the person has to put their name behind you.

---

## Ask, do not sell

No metrics anywhere. No CAC figures, no download counts, no ROAS. Those live on the CV,
and a message that reads as a pitch is the one that gets ignored.

Never close with a challenge or an ultimatum ("is that requirement firm?").

Where they do not meet a requirement, **name it plainly and move on.** That reads as
honest. Do not follow it with an apology, and do not downplay real experience to seem
humble — if something was built for clients, say so.

Ground every message in the actual posting and that person's specific remit. A media
director and a sponsorship director do not get the same text.

---

## Read every draft against these before showing it

The tells that keep slipping through, from
[Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing):

- **Negative parallelism** — "not just X but Y", "rather than X, Y". The worst offender.
- **Rule of three** — three parallel clauses, three blocks, three-item lists.
- **Labelled blocks** in something that should be continuous prose.
- **Copula avoidance** — "serves as", "represents", "marks" instead of "is".
- **Marketing verbs** — "boasts", "features", "showcases" instead of "has".
- **Undue significance** — "pivotal", "crucial", "underscores", "speaks to".
- **Em dashes.** Use commas, full stops or brackets.
- **Flagged words** — delve, landscape, tapestry, testament, robust, meticulous,
  intricate, vibrant, leverage, align with, foster, garner, enhance.
- **Clipped fragment closers** for effect. Say the thing and stop.
- **Formulaic openers reused across messages.** "From outside it looks like..." is fine
  once. Four times in a week is a signature.

What reads human: plain verbs, contractions, approximations ("20-odd", "about seven
months"), one idea per sentence, and no summarising flourish at the end.

**Self-check before delivering.** Grep the draft for the flagged list, count em dashes,
count words, and confirm no referral ask. A three-line script beats re-reading it.
